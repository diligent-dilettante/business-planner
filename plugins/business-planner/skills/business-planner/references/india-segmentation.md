# India Segmentation — reference (MBB-style)

A consultant-grade toolkit for cutting the Indian market **many ways** — because "the Indian consumer" is a fiction and opportunity hides in specific slices. Use this to (a) **spot opportunity** (find an underserved, reachable, willing-to-pay slice) and (b) **check fit** (is the founder's actual first customer a real, sized, reachable segment?).

**How to use:** don't recite all cuts. Pick the 4–6 dimensions that matter for *this* business, intersect two or three of them to name a precise beachhead (e.g. "India1 + millennial new-parent + Tier-1 + convenience-led" or "India1 + senior + metro + health/service-led" or "top-India2 + Gen Z + wellness-led + q-comm-native"), then size it bottom-up. **Every age is a market** — don't default to the young; often the biggest wallet is a millennial peak-earner or an under-served Gen X / senior. A precise intersection of three cuts is worth more than one broad label. Always end on: *is this slice big enough, reachable, and willing to pay — and can this founder serve it?*

Figures are directional and dated (2025–26). Web-search to confirm any specific number before locking it.

## The 30 cuts

### A. Economic class (the spine — start here)
1. **India1 / India2 / India3 / India0** — the Blume lens. India1 (~120–140M, ~$15K/capita, premium paying market), India2 (~300M, ~$3K, affordable-premium aspirational), India3 (~1B, hard to monetize), India0 (~35M diaspora, $100B+ remittances, premium/nostalgia). *Most viable cut for a ₹1–2M-USD physical business: India1 + top-of-India2.*
2. **Income band** — HNI / mass-affluent / emerging-middle / aspirer / next-billion. Finer than India1/2/3 when pricing.
3. **Spending posture** — premiumizing (trading up), value-conscious (trading down/steady), K-shaped split within the same category (SUV up, entry car down).

### B. Geography — each tier is its own microcosm (not just a size ladder)
*Metro / Tier-1 / Tier-2 aren't points on a wealth line — each has its own competitive intensity, real estate economics, talent pool, channel structure, and consumer psychology. A plan that works in one can fail in another. Load `references/india-regional-tiers.md` for the full picture; the essentials:*
4. **Metro / Tier-1** — highest incomes and premium demand, but **saturated and hyper-competitive**: peak rents, peak ad-CAC, peak talent costs *and* talent attrition (~25%), crowded shelves, and a growing "ghost mall" problem (~60 malls with 40%+ vacancy from dated retail). Win here on brand/differentiation, not on being cheap. Real-estate rental yields are low (2.5–3.5%) — a physical footprint is expensive.
5. **Tier-2 — the current frontier, and its own world** — office leasing ~doubled YoY (FY25); rents 30–50% lower than metros; attrition drops to <10%; **Grade-A retail is now higher in Tier-2 (~61%) than Tier-1 (~45%)** as international brands and malls move in. Rental yields higher (5–6.5% in parts of Hyderabad; Chandigarh has India's highest urban MPCE at ~₹13,425). Value-conscious but rising, family-oriented, trust-and-relationship-led, vernacular-first. *No longer a "test market" — a primary market. But infrastructure is uneven and each city is distinct (Coimbatore ≠ Lucknow ≠ Bhubaneswar).*
6. **Tier-3 / rural** — value-led, price-sensitive, WOM- and trust-driven, vernacular-dominant, offline + newly-online (JioPhone); huge but hard to serve profitably (CAC/logistics). Distribution and trust, not premium, win here.
7. **Region / zone** — North / South / East / West / Northeast differ sharply in taste, language, festival calendar, savings/gold behaviour, channel structure, and brand loyalty. **South India is a distinct economic and cultural bloc** — highest per-capita consumption, vernacular-first, strong regional retail champions — see `references/india-regional-tiers.md`.
8. **State-level** — GST implementation, licensing, retail structure, language, and political/policy climate vary by state; a physical footprint or supply chain is a state-by-state decision, not a national one.
9. **Gated community / apartment cluster** — a real distribution unit in urban India (q-comm density, RWAs, bulk/subscription); "gated India" is its own beachhead.
10. **Urban informal / peri-urban** — migrant, dense, cash-and-UPI, underserved by premium retail.

### C. Generation & life stage
*Every generation is a market. Gen Z is influential and growing, but millennials are in peak earning years now, and Gen X / boomers / seniors often hold the highest disposable income and are less contested. Don't default to young — pick the cohort that fits the business.*
11. **Gen Z (born ~1997–2012, ~400M in India)** — large and influential, drives ~44–48% of spend (much of it influenced/parental), digital-first discovery, fluid loyalty, values-led — but not yet peak earners. *See india-consumption-by-age.md.*
12. **Millennials (born ~1981–1996)** — peak earners *now*, largest wallet in many categories; convenience + family-led premiumization; still on Facebook/YouTube more than Gen Z; blend online discovery with offline buying. Often the real money for a premium physical product.
13. **Gen X (born ~1965–1980)** — established careers, high disposable income, family decision-makers (school, home, health, auto); value + trust led; brand-loyal once earned; underserved by youth-obsessed marketing — a quiet opportunity.
14. **Boomers / seniors (60+) — the silver economy** — growing fast as India ages; high trust-and-service expectations; health, wellness, leisure, assisted living, travel, and convenience spend; often cash-and-family-supported; heavily *under*-served by startups chasing the young. A real and widening white space.
15. **Gen Alpha (born 2013+, via parents)** — kidswear, edtech, toys, nutrition; parent is the buyer, child the influencer; screen-native from birth.
16. **Life stage (often predicts need better than age)** — student / first-job / newly-married / new-parent / established-family / empty-nester / retiree. A new-parent of any generation may be the truer segment than "millennial."

### D. Digital & channel behaviour
17. **Discovery mode** — social-first (Instagram/YouTube reels), search-first (Google/Amazon), voice/vernacular search, word-of-mouth/offline. Skews social among the young, search/WOM/offline among older — but plenty of older buyers are now social-active and many young buy in-store; check, don't assume.
18. **Channel-native** — q-commerce-native (10-min impulse), marketplace-loyal (Amazon/Flipkart/Meesho), D2C-website, social-commerce/livestream buyer, offline-only, phygital (discover online, buy in store — still ~majority for apparel).
19. **Payment behaviour** — UPI-default, BNPL-user (Gen Z skews here), COD-reliant (trust-gap markets), card/EMI.
20. **Digital access tier** — smartphone-rich metro / JioPhone-enabled rural newly-online / feature-phone / offline.

### E. Attitude & values (psychographic)
21. **Premium-signal seekers** — buy for status/social currency (Gen Z mid-premium; India1 luxury).
22. **Health/wellness-led** — clean-label, functional, preventive; the fastest-rising attitude cut across F&B, BPC, fitness.
23. **Values-led** — sustainability, ethics, transparency, local/"vocal for local"; strongest in the young (~47% of Indian Gen Z pay a premium for sustainable) but rising across ages.
24. **Convenience-maximalist** — time-poor, will pay for speed/delivery/done-for-me.
25. **Experience-over-ownership** — spend on travel, events, experiences, rentals over goods (Gen Z shift).
26. **Regional-proud / heritage** — want local taste, language, identity woven in ("proudly regional" — Fireside's 2030 consumer).
27. **Trust-scarce / skeptical** — won't buy without social proof, reviews, referral, or a trusted brand (~79% of Asian EM consumers only buy trusted brands).

### F. Category-behaviour
28. **Purchase frequency** — daily-consumable (built-in repeat: milk, food, coffee) vs. considered/durable (one-off: furniture, appliance). Repeat-built categories have a structural retention edge.
29. **Loyalty posture** — loyalist / promiscuous-switcher / price-driven. Gen Z is loyal *and* promiscuous — loves brands but readily tries competitors.
30. **Basket role** — hero purchase vs. add-on/impulse (q-comm impulse is a distinct behaviour to design for).

## Opportunity-spotting workflow

1. **Fix the spine:** which economic class? (Usually India1 / top-India2 for a premium physical business.)
2. **Intersect 2–3 more cuts** to name a precise slice — e.g. *India1 × millennial new-parent × metro × convenience-led*, or *India1 × senior × Tier-1 × health/service-led*, or *top-India2 × Gen Z × wellness-led × q-comm-native.* Pick the age that fits the business, not the trendy one.
3. **Test the slice against four screens:**
   - **Sized?** Bottom-up: reachable buyers × price × frequency. Real, not "1% of 1.4 billion."
   - **Underserved?** Who serves them today, how badly? (White space = a job done poorly.)
   - **Reachable?** A channel that hits them at a cost that leaves margin.
   - **Willing to pay?** Real WTP for *this* slice, not a converted average.
4. **Rank slices** by size × ease-of-reach × margin, then pick a **beachhead** (win one completely — Crossing the Chasm) before widening.

## Fit-check workflow (for a founder who already named a customer)

- Restate their customer as a precise intersection of cuts. If they can't be pinned to 3+ cuts, the target is too vague — that's the finding.
- Check the four screens above against their claim.
- Cross-check **founder-fit**: can *this* founder actually reach and serve *this* slice? (An India1-premium slice needs brand/design/trust the founder may not have; a Tier-2 value slice needs distribution muscle.)
- Feed the named slice into **market-sim** as the panel definition, and into customer **stories**.

## Bias watch (segmentation-specific)

- **Everyone-is-my-customer** — the fatal non-answer. Force an intersection.
- **1% of 1.4 billion** — India TAM inflation. Only India1 + top-India2 is reliably monetizable for premium physical goods.
- **Metro goggles** — designing for Bangalore/Mumbai and assuming Tier-2/3 follows identically.
- **Averaging** — collapsing a varied population to a mean persona; the mean customer often doesn't exist. Keep the variation.
- **Gen Z monolith** — Gen Z is not one segment; intersect with class, region, and values.
- **Youth-default / shiny-cohort bias** — assuming the young are *the* market. Millennials are peak earners now; Gen X and seniors hold high disposable income and are under-served. Pick the cohort with the wallet and the need, not the one in the trend pieces.
- **Survivorship in cuts** — copying the slice a famous brand won without checking if it's still open or was luck.
