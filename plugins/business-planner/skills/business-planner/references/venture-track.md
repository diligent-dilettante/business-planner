# Venture Track — reference (Venture profile only)

**Load only when the ambition profile is Venture.** Never apply this to a livelihood or growth business — it would impose scale pressure where it doesn't belong. This is the extra rigor a world-class, VC-track founder of a *physical/consumer* business needs, layered on top of the normal sections.

The core discipline stays: any capital must return more than its cost, and **raising is selling the future for speed — only worth it if speed wins the category.**

---

## 1. The venture-scale test (run before accepting the Venture label)

A Venture plan must clear a higher bar than founder ambition. Pressure-test against what makes a *physical/consumer* business venture-fundable:
- **Category size & growth** — is the addressable category large and growing (bottom-up, not "1% of India")?
- **Gross margin** — typically **>50–60%** to fund CAC, retention, and a brand. Food/grocery is structurally lower (35–55%) — harder venture case; BPC/wellness easier.
- **Repeat / retention** — daily-consumable or subscription (built-in repeat) beats one-off durables. Investors buy the repeat curve.
- **Moat beyond recipe** — brand, cornered supply, process, or data — not a formula a competitor copies in months.
- **Cash-conversion cycle** — inventory + receivables shouldn't strangle growth; physical scale eats cash.
- **House-of-brands / category-leadership path** — a credible way to be #1 in a category or a multi-brand platform.

If it fails several: it's a strong **Growth** business, not Venture. Say so — that's not a downgrade, it's honesty. (See the upward/downward scale-bias rule in the controller.)

## 2. Fundraising sequence (physical/consumer, India)

- **Bootstrap → Angel → Seed → Series A → B+.** Each round buys specific milestones; don't raise ahead of proof.
- **Seed** proves repeat + unit economics (not just revenue). **Series A** scales a *nailed* model. Raising A on an un-nailed model burns faster, not better.
- Indian consumer-focused funds (Fireside, Blume, DSG, Sauce-type, and generalists) each have theses — match the fund to the category. *Verify current active funds/theses by search.*
- **Milestone-per-round discipline:** state what each raise must achieve (traction, margin, channel, geography) and what the next round needs to see.

## 3. Dilution & cap-table math (model it, don't hand-wave)

- Each priced round typically trades **~15–25%** for capital + speed. Founders can dip under 50% ownership by Series B — plan for it.
- ESOP pool (10–15%) dilutes founders too; carve it consciously.
- **The real question:** does the capital buy enough speed/scale to make a smaller slice of a bigger pie worth more than a larger slice of a self-funded one? Often yes for winner-take-most categories, often no otherwise.
- Model best/base/worst cap tables to the plausible exit; show founders their ownership at exit under each.

## 4. Burn & runway (first-class for Venture)

- Burn rate, runway (months of cash), and the "default alive vs default dead" line are core metrics, not footnotes.
- Physical adds inventory burn — cash locked in stock is real burn even if it's an "asset."
- Every raise should extend runway past the *next* proof milestone with buffer, not just to the milestone.

## 5. Exit landscape (the actual endgame in India)

- **Strategic acquisition is the dominant outcome:** HUL, Marico, ITC, Tata Consumer, Nestlé, Reliance, and roll-up platforms buy proven Indian consumer brands. Build to be *acquirable* — clean books, defensible brand, category position.
- **IPO** is the exception (Honasa/Mamaearth, Nykaa, pending listings) — real but rare; don't make it the plan.
- **Acqui-hire / distressed sale** is the downside — plan so it's not the only door.
- Name a plausible acquirer and what they'd pay for, in year 5–7, from day one. It shapes what you build.

## 6. Venture grill add-ons
- "Is this genuinely venture-scale on category/margin/repeat/moat — or Growth in a Venture costume?"
- "What does each round buy, what does the next need to see, and what's your ownership at exit?"
- "Who acquires this in 5–7 years, and what are you building now that makes them pay?"
- "Are you default-alive without the next round — or betting the company on raising?"
