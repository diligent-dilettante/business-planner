# MSME Setup On-Ramp — reference

The first-time MSME/livelihood founder doesn't have a lawyer or CA on call — this skill is their on-ramp. Venture founders have advisors; this file is for the shop, kitchen, unit, or services founder who needs to actually *get set up* to trade legally in India. The planner grills on compliance *risk*; this file helps them *do it*. Directional and dated (2025–26) — **re-verify thresholds, fees, and steps by web-search before stating them; rules change.**

Keep it concise and jargon-light (skip MBA-speak; the founder reads English fine and doesn't need terms spelled out — they need brevity). This is a checklist, not tax advice — for anything complex, point them to a CA.

---

## The first-week checklist (typical sequence)

1. **Decide the legal form.** Sole proprietorship (simplest, cheapest, personal liability), partnership, LLP, or Private Limited (needed if raising equity later). Most livelihood businesses start as proprietorship; a growth/venture one usually needs Pvt Ltd. *One-way-door-ish — switching later is work, but proprietorship→Pvt Ltd is common.*
2. **Udyam registration (MSME).** Free, online, self-declared (Aadhaar + PAN). This is the **key that unlocks** priority-sector loans, collateral-free schemes (CGTMSE/Mudra), subsidies, and tender eligibility. Do it early — most of `india-capital-landscape.md` depends on it.
3. **PAN + current account.** Business PAN (or proprietor's PAN for a proprietorship) and a **current account** (not a savings account — banks require business proof; keep business and personal money separate from day one, it saves pain at tax time and signals seriousness to lenders).
4. **GST registration.** Mandatory above the turnover threshold, and **effectively mandatory the moment you sell on any e-commerce/q-commerce platform** regardless of turnover. Registration also lets you claim input tax credit (ITC). A simplified pan-India registration exists for small e-commerce sellers. *Verify current thresholds.*
5. **Category licenses (only what applies):**
   - **FSSAI** — any food business (even a home kitchen/cloud kitchen; cloud kitchen also needs Fire NOC).
   - **BIS** — many manufactured goods (mandatory standards for certain products).
   - **Drug & Cosmetic license** — BPC/cosmetics/supplements.
   - **Shops & Establishment registration** — most physical premises (state-level).
   - **Trade license / municipal permits, Fire NOC, pollution consent** — depending on premises and activity.
   - **Import-Export Code (IEC)** — only if importing/exporting.
6. **Trademark (brand protection).** File the brand name/logo early if the brand matters — cheap insurance against squatting; especially before spending on packaging/marketing.
7. **Basic bookkeeping + GST filing rhythm.** Set up monthly GST reconciliation from the start (mismatches and missed ITC are a common, avoidable cash leak). A simple accounting tool + a part-time CA beats a year-end scramble.

## What each unlocks (so the founder sees why)
- **Udyam** → loans, schemes, subsidies, tenders.
- **GST** → sell on platforms, claim ITC, invoice B2B customers.
- **Current account** → lender credibility, clean books, tax ease.
- **FSSAI/BIS/licenses** → legal to sell; avoids shutdown/penalty (put lead time on the critical path — some take weeks/months).
- **Trademark** → owns the brand you're building.

## Common first-timer mistakes (flag these)
- Mixing personal and business money (no current account) — wrecks bookkeeping and lender trust.
- Skipping Udyam and then wondering why loans are hard.
- Treating GST as a year-end task → notices, penalties, lost ITC. It's a monthly discipline.
- Starting to sell food/cosmetics before the license lands — a shutdown risk.
- Not registering the trademark until after a competitor does.

## Grill / help add-ons
- "Which of these do you actually need for *your* category — and which are on the critical path (license lead times) before you can sell?"
- "Is Udyam done? It gates most of your capital options."
- "Business and personal money separated yet (current account)?"
- "For anything complex (structure, big GST, IP), have you got a CA/CS — or should we keep it simple for now?"

*Thresholds, fees, portal steps, and license requirements are directional and dated — re-verify by web-search, and send the founder to a CA/CS for anything non-routine. This is an on-ramp, not tax or legal advice.*
