# Domestic → International Conversion

**Apply any time the plan touches a second country** — exporting product, importing supply, or launching abroad. Core rule: **a domestic plan does not transfer. Re-validate demand, economics, ops, and law in the new market from scratch.** Flag the "silent transfer" assumption — believing what worked at home works abroad unchanged — as its own bias every time.

## 1. Demand & product-market fit (re-validate, don't assume)
- Is the *problem* even the same abroad? Local habits, climate, home sizes, sport culture, price sensitivity differ.
- Does the product need to change — dimensions, materials, voltage, language, sizing, taste, certification marks?
- Who is the local first customer, and what's the *local* bottom-up SOM? (Not "it worked at home, so 10% of Country X.")
- Local demand proof, not extrapolation: local search, local pre-orders, a local design partner.
- **Bias watch:** narrative transfer ("brand loved here = loved there"), availability (one viral foreign example).

## 2. Unit economics abroad (rebuild the P&L)
- **Landed cost changes completely:** freight (sea vs air), insurance, import duty/tariff (HS code), customs clearance, port/demurrage, local last-mile.
- **FX:** which currency do you cost in vs price in? Who carries the FX risk and over what lag? Model a ±10–15% swing.
- **Pricing:** local willingness-to-pay and competitor price bands, not a converted domestic price. Account for local taxes (VAT/GST/sales tax) in the shelf price.
- **CAC & margins** differ by market maturity and channel mix — don't reuse domestic numbers.
- **Repatriation:** how does profit come home, at what tax and transfer cost?
- **Bias watch:** optimism on freight/duty, forgetting FX and repatriation drag.

## 3. Supply chain & logistics
- Import vs local sourcing decision for the new market. Dual-sourcing to hedge single-country risk.
- Lead times balloon across borders — plan for customs delays, longer MOQs, higher inventory buffers (more cash locked).
- Warehousing/fulfilment: 3PL in-country vs cross-border ship? Returns/reverse logistics across a border are expensive and slow — plan explicitly.
- Incoterms clarity (who owns goods/cost/risk at each leg: EXW, FOB, CIF, DDP).
- **Bias watch:** planning-fallacy on cross-border lead times, ignoring reverse logistics.

## 4. Regulatory, legal, tax, compliance
- Product certifications/standards for the destination (safety, materials, labelling). Getting these can take months — put on the critical path.
- Import licenses, restricted/prohibited goods lists, tariff classification.
- Entity & tax: do you need a local entity, distributor, or can you sell cross-border directly? Permanent-establishment and tax implications.
- IP: is the brand/design protected in the new market, or is it already squatted?
- Data/consumer-protection law if selling online (e.g. GDPR-type regimes).
- Payments: local payment methods, settlement, and FX on collection.
- **Bias watch:** regulatory optimism, assuming home compliance = abroad compliance.

## 5. Go-to-market & distribution
- Channel that wins at home may not exist or dominate abroad (marketplace share, retail structure, D2C penetration all vary).
- Local trust, reviews, and brand-building start from zero — budget for it.
- Distributor/importer vs direct: faster but margin-diluting and control-losing. Decide deliberately.
- Local partnerships, language, and support. Positioning and message need local testing, not translation.
- **Bias watch:** copying the domestic playbook, "online = borderless."

## 6. Operating model & organisation
- Who runs it in-country — local hire, partner, or remote? Timezone and language load.
- Cash and working-capital needs are higher abroad (longer cash-conversion cycle). Fund it before you commit.
- **One-way-door:** entering some markets (entity setup, long supplier contracts, certification spend) is costly to reverse. Size the cost of failure before committing; prefer a reversible pilot (small test shipment, one city, one channel) first.

## The four questions to force before any international move
1. **Re-validated locally?** Have we proven demand and economics *in that market*, not extrapolated from home?
2. **Landed cost + FX + tax modelled?** Do we have a real local P&L with a currency-swing stress test?
3. **Compliance on the critical path?** Do we know the certifications/licenses and their lead time before we launch?
4. **Reversible pilot first?** Are we testing small and reversible before an irreversible bet?
