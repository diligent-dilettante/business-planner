# India Capital Landscape — reference

For physical businesses, **getting** capital is often the binding constraint, not planning it. This maps India's actual funding sources for both the MSME/livelihood founder (loans, schemes, credit) and the venture founder (equity stages). Use it in the financial-plan sections to turn "where's the money from?" into a concrete, named answer. Directional and dated (2025–26) — **re-verify limits, rates, and eligibility by web-search before quoting them; scheme terms change often.**

The principle from the planner still governs: capital must return more than its cost. Debt has a rate to beat; equity has a dilution cost. Name the source, its true cost, and whether the business can service it.

## PART A — MSME / livelihood / self-funded (debt & schemes)

Most Indian physical businesses at ₹0→₹17 crore are funded by **debt, schemes, and self/family capital — not equity.** The map:

- **Own + family capital** — the default first source; cheapest to control, but real cost = the return you forgo elsewhere (FD, gold, index) and household risk. Size the max loss you can personally survive.
- **Mudra loans (PMMY)** — collateral-free loans for micro/small non-farm businesses via banks/NBFCs/MFIs, in tiers (historically Shishu / Kishore / Tarun, with a higher Tarun band added). Good for equipment, working capital, small setup. *Verify current ceilings and tiers.*
- **CGTMSE** — credit-guarantee scheme that lets banks lend to micro/small enterprises **without collateral** (the trust guarantees a portion). The reason a first-timer with no assets can still get a term/working-capital loan. *Verify current cover limits.*
- **PSB / bank MSME loans** — term loans + cash-credit/overdraft working-capital lines from public and private banks; **Udyam registration** (see `msme-setup.md`) unlocks priority-sector lending and better terms.
- **Stand-Up India / PMEGP / state schemes** — targeted schemes (women/SC-ST founders, manufacturing, subsidy-linked). State MSME/industries departments run their own incentives (rent/power/capital subsidy) — check the specific state.
- **Gold loan** — fast, collateral-based, culturally normal (esp. South India); the **informal hurdle rate to beat** — if the business returns less than a gold loan costs, rethink.
- **Supplier / trade credit** — the cheapest working capital: net-30/60 terms from suppliers. Underused lever; negotiate it explicitly (ties to stakeholder stories).
- **TReDS** — a digital platform to discount receivables (get paid early on invoices to large buyers) — eases the working-capital gap that kills physical businesses.
- **NBFC / fintech loans** — faster but pricier than banks; useful bridge, watch the rate.

**MSME grill:** which of these, at what rate, and can monthly cash flow service it through a slow season? Is Udyam done (it gates most of the above)? Is supplier credit and TReDS being used before taking a costly loan?

## PART B — Growth / venture (equity stages)

For founders genuinely on a venture path (large category, fast scale, outside investors). Physical/consumer businesses raise differently from SaaS — investors scrutinise gross margin, repeat rate, and inventory/cash cycle hard.

**The stage ladder (India, consumer/physical):**
- **Bootstrap / revenue** — proving the model before dilution; strongest position.
- **Angel / friends-family** — small first cheque for early inventory/team; individuals or angel networks.
- **Seed** — first institutional round; consumer-focused seed funds (e.g. Fireside, Blume, DSG-type) back brands with early traction. They want proof of repeat and unit economics, not just revenue.
- **Series A** — scale a *proven* model (repeatable acquisition, healthy margins, real retention). "Nail it then scale it" — A money on an un-nailed model just loses faster.
- **Series B+** — category expansion, house-of-brands, geographic scale.

**What Indian consumer VCs actually look for** (use as the venture-scale test): large and growing category; **gross margin typically >50–60%** (hard in food, easier in BPC); genuine **repeat/retention** (daily-consumable > one-off); a **brand/moat** that isn't just a copyable recipe; sane **inventory/cash-conversion cycle**; and a credible **house-of-brands or category-leadership** path.

**Dilution reality (say this plainly to venture founders):** every round trades ownership for capital and speed. Rough intuition — each priced round often gives up ~15–25%; founders can hold well under half by Series B. Raising is not free money; it's selling the future in exchange for going faster. Only worth it if speed wins the category. *Model the cap table, don't hand-wave it.*

**The exit landscape (the real venture endgame in India):** most consumer-brand outcomes are **strategic acquisitions** — HUL, Marico, ITC, Tata, Nestlé, Reliance, and roll-ups (Honasa, Mensa-type) buy proven Indian brands — plus a handful of IPOs (Mamaearth/Honasa, Nykaa, and pending listings). Build toward being *acquirable*: clean books, defensible brand, category leadership. IPO is the exception, not the plan.

**Venture grill:** is this genuinely venture-scale (category, margin, repeat, moat), or Growth wearing a Venture costume? What's the raise, the dilution, the milestones each round buys, and the plausible acquirer in 5–7 years? (Load `venture-track.md` for the full treatment.)

## Cross-cutting

- **Debt vs equity:** debt keeps ownership but must be serviced monthly (deadly in a slow season); equity shares risk but costs control and future upside. Match to cash-flow shape and ambition.
- **Cost of capital is the bar** either way: a livelihood loan must return more than its interest + your time; venture equity must return more than the dilution and the alternative uses of your years.
- **Working capital first:** before raising *anything*, exhaust supplier credit, TReDS, and inventory discipline — the cheapest capital is the cash you don't lock up.
