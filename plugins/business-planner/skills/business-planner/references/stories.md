# Stories Engine — reference

The plan is accountable to **stories**: short, strict-template statements that capture what each actor needs, does, and won't do. Borrowed from the PM user-story discipline — the unit of shared understanding — extended to the three actors a physical business lives or dies on: **customers, the founder(s), and other stakeholders.**

Stories are captured **inline** as each section locks, and **collected** in one place (plan §0.5 Stories Ledger + template's Stories section). Draft them **silently** from the founder's grill answers — don't interrogate story-by-story — but also invite the founder to volunteer their own. Spread the effort so no single moment exhausts them.

---

## The strict template (all story types)

> **As a** [actor], **when** [situation / trigger], **I want** [job to be done], **so that** [outcome], **and I'd** [observable action: buy / pay ₹X / switch / refer / supply / invest / walk].

The final **"and I'd…"** clause is non-negotiable — it forces an *observable action*, which is what makes a story testable rather than a wish. A story with no action clause is a hope, not a story.

Each story also carries a one-word **status**: `assumed` (from grill, unverified) · `sim-tested` (ran through market-sim, still a hypothesis) · `evidence` (backed by a real signal — pre-order, signed supplier, LOI). Status makes the plan's epistemics visible at a glance.

---

## 1. Customer stories

What the customer is hiring the business for and what they'd actually do. These are the same stories market-sim runs against a synthetic panel — draft them here, test them there, upgrade status when real evidence arrives.

- Draft during **A1–A3 / B1–B3** (bet, market, competition).
- One per meaningful segment (India1 / top-of-India2 / regional niche for India plans — not "the customer").
- Example: *As a **premium home-cook in India1**, when **my old cookware wears out**, I want **durable non-toxic cookware that signals taste**, so that **I cook proudly and replace less often**, and I'd **pay ₹4,000 but balk at ₹7,000**.* `assumed`

## 2. Founder stories

The founder's honest narrative of fit — why them, what they've lived, what they can execute vs. must get help with. Ties directly to the **founder-fit** sub-skill. Not self-congratulation: each names a real capability and its limit.

- Draft during **founder-fit** (post A3 / B2), plus the warm-up hard dos/don'ts.
- Cover: domain edge, network, hard skill, and at least one honest gap.
- Example: *As the **founder**, because **I ran a restaurant for 8 years and know food supply chains**, I want to **own sourcing and QC directly**, so that **quality stays high and costs stay tight**, and I'd **run this confidently without a hire**.* `evidence`
- Gap example: *As the **founder**, because **I've never run paid acquisition**, I want to **bring in a performance-marketing partner early**, so that **CAC doesn't quietly sink the unit economics**, and I'd **budget ₹X/month or give equity for it**.* `assumed`

## 3. Stakeholder stories

Everyone else with a stake — a physical business runs on these relationships. Identify them explicitly; each gets a story capturing what *they* need, fear, and will do. Missing stakeholders are a common blind spot.

Typical stakeholders to check for (prompt the founder through the list, skip the irrelevant):
- **Co-founder / partner** — split, roles, what happens if it goes wrong.
- **Suppliers / manufacturers** — MOQ, terms, exclusivity, what makes them prioritize you.
- **Distributors / retailers / marketplace or q-comm platforms** — margin, shelf, listing terms.
- **Employees / first hires** — the critical early role and why they'd join a small unproven business.
- **Funders** — bank, family, gold-loan, angel, investor: what return/security they need, what they fear.
- **Landlord / location** — lease terms, the one-way-door risk of a physical footprint.
- **Delivery / logistics partners** — reliability, cost, reverse logistics.
- **Regulators / certifiers** — FSSAI/BIS/GST etc.: what they require, timeline, what non-compliance costs.
- **Community / neighbours / local ecosystem** — for a physical location, local goodwill is real.

- Draft during **A6–A8 / B5–B7** (ops, GTM, regulatory) and financial plan (funders).
- Example: *As a **key supplier**, when **a small new brand approaches me**, I want **reliable repeat orders and on-time payment**, so that **I prioritise their production over bigger sporadic buyers**, and I'd **offer better terms once they hit ₹X monthly volume**.* `assumed`

## Front-loaded founder inputs (do this early, once)

At the **start** — during or right after ambition calibration, before deep section grilling — capture three quick founder inputs. Ask for them directly (the founder types them), then the skill folds them into stories and grilling. Keep it light; this is the one place you ask the founder to volunteer, not answer 20 questions.

1. **Top 3–5 things that must be true for this to work** (the founder's own bets). → become `assumed` customer/stakeholder stories to test.
2. **Hard DOs** — 3–5 lines the founder is committed to no matter what ("must stay founder-owned," "quality never compromised," "no debt"). → become guardrails; flag any later plan step that violates one.
3. **Hard DON'Ts** — 3–5 explicit lines the business will not cross ("won't sell on deep-discount marketplaces," "won't scale before margins work," "won't take investor money"). → become non-goals + guardrails.

Hard DOs/DON'Ts are **binding constraints**: if a later section's plan violates one, stop and surface it — don't quietly override it. If the founder chooses to change a hard rule, log it in the Override Log with the reason.

## Capture rules (how the grill produces stories)

1. **Silent drafting.** As a section's grill answers come in, draft the relevant stories yourself. Don't turn the interview into "now give me a story for X" — that exhausts people.
2. **Invite, don't force.** Once early, ask the founder for their top 3–5 must-be-trues and hard dos/don'ts in their own words. Elsewhere, only ask for a story if a critical actor is a total blank.
3. **Surface at lock.** When a section locks, show the 1–3 stories it produced, in template form, for a quick confirm/edit. This is the checkpoint — cheap, not exhausting.
4. **Spread the load.** Customer stories at A1–A3, founder stories at founder-fit, stakeholder stories at ops/GTM/regulatory/finance. Never all at once.
5. **Status honesty.** Default every new story to `assumed`. Upgrade to `sim-tested` only after market-sim, `evidence` only after a real-world signal. Never let `assumed` masquerade as `evidence`.
6. **Collect.** Maintain the running **Stories Ledger** (plan §0.5) so all three types sit in one view, each with its status — the plan's honest map of who it's betting on and how sure it is.

---

## Why three actors, not one

A PRD had users. A physical business has three failure surfaces: the **customer** won't buy, the **founder** can't execute, or a **stakeholder** (supplier, landlord, funder, regulator) breaks the chain. A story for each makes all three testable and visible, instead of only pressure-testing demand while an unnamed supplier or an unaffordable lease quietly sinks the plan.
